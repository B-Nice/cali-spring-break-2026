Day 1Wed, Mar 24

Arrive in San Francisco

Meet at SFO

Late morningTwo rental cars

Delta from Detroit lands at 10:43am and United from Denver at 11:25am, so both families can meet at the rental car center before noon. Nine people with luggage means two cars (or one 12-passenger van). Book them as one-way rentals that drop off at Fresno, and compare the one-way fees.

Presidio and Crissy Field

1–2 hrsFlat, stroller-friendlyFree

A former army post turned national park site, with eucalyptus groves, brick barracks and big bay views. Crissy Field is its waterfront: a wide, flat promenade of roughly 1.5 miles to Fort Point, with a beach and the bridge at the far end. Good for stretching legs after a flight, and the cousins can run on the grass and sand. The Presidio Tunnel Tops park above the roadway has a play area if the kids need a longer break.

Golden Gate Bridge

30–60 minWalkable, 1.7 mi spanWindy

You can walk onto the east-side sidewalk for a taste of the span. Fort Point (under the south end) and Battery East are the classic photo angles. Bring a layer, because wind and fog can make it much cooler than the rest of the city.

Settle in early

Evening

Stay near the Presidio or on the Marin side so tomorrow's Muir Woods start is short. Day 2 is the longest drive of the trip.

Drive: SFO to SF hotel, about 25 min

Day 2Thu, Mar 25

Muir Woods, then east to Yosemite

Muir Woods National Monument

2–3 hrs0.5–2.5 mi loopsReservations required

Old-growth coast redwoods, some more than 250 feet tall and over 600 years old, line a flat boardwalk and gravel path along Redwood Creek. The shortest loop is about half a mile; the main-trail loop runs roughly 2 miles via Cathedral Grove. Parking or the shuttle must be reserved ahead, and cell service is poor inside, so download the reservation. A Thursday visit should be far calmer than a weekend.

Marin Headlands (optional)

45–60 minRoadside viewsSkip if tired

Cliffs and open hills at the north end of the Golden Gate. Battery Spencer is the well-known bridge view, a short walk from the road. Worth it only if the group has energy before a 4-hour drive.

The drive to Yosemite

~4–4.5 hrsMuir Woods to Mariposa

Through the East Bay, across the Central Valley and up into the Sierra foothills to Mariposa, about an hour from the Valley. Fill up and buy snacks before the foothills. With two cars, agree on a lunch stop so you don't lose each other.

Drive: SF to Muir Woods 40 min · Muir Woods to Mariposa 4 to 4.5 hrs · about 5 hrs total

Day 3Fri, Mar 26

Yosemite Valley

Tunnel View

15–30 minRoadside, no hikeFree

The classic first look: El Capitan on the left, Bridalveil Fall on the right and Half Dome in the distance. It is a pullout at the east end of the Wawona Tunnel, so it works for everyone. Spring snowmelt usually makes the falls strong this time of year. Go early; it gets crowded.

Bridalveil Fall

~20–30 minAbout 0.5 mi round tripEasy, paved

A short paved walk to the base of a 620-foot fall. In spring the spray can soak you, so bring rain shells. The path can be slick or patchy with snow.

Lower Yosemite Fall

~30 minAbout 1 mi loopFlat, paved

A gentle loop to the base of the lower fall, part of the tallest waterfall in North America (2,425 feet in three stages). Late March through May is normally when it runs best.

Cook's Meadow and the Valley floor

30–60 minAbout 1 miFlat

A flat boardwalk loop with wide views of Half Dome, Yosemite Falls and Sentinel Rock. The Yosemite Village visitor center and Ansel Adams Gallery are close by for a break or a snack.

Drive: Mariposa to the Valley about 1 hr each way · Valley stops 5 to 15 min apart

Day 4Sat, Mar 27

Giant sequoias at Mariposa Grove

Valley morning (flex)

2–3 hrsPick one

Whatever you missed or liked best: the paved path from Happy Isles toward the Vernal Fall footbridge (about 0.8 mi round trip to the bridge), Mirror Lake (about 2 miles round trip, mostly flat), or a ranger program. Saturday will be busier, so start early.

Mariposa Grove of Giant Sequoias

2–3 hrsUp to 2+ mi each wayAccess depends on snow

More than 500 mature giant sequoias, including the Grizzly Giant (likely 2,000+ years old) and the California Tunnel Tree, cut through in 1895. The shuttle does not start until at least April 15, so in late March plan to walk about 2 miles each way from the lower lot. Budget half a day and bring water and snacks. Snow may linger on shady trails.

Where to sleep

Oakhurst or Fish Camp

Staying south of the park cuts tomorrow's drive. Book early: this is the Saturday before Easter.

Drive: about 2.5 to 3 hrs total including the Valley round trip
Glacier Point Road is usually closed this early. Use the time in the Valley instead.

Day 5Sun, Mar 28 · Easter

South to Sequoia National Park

The drive south

3–3.5 hrsVia Fresno and CA-180

Down Highway 41 to Fresno, then up CA-180 into Kings Canyon and south on the Generals Highway to Giant Forest. The last stretch is slow and winding. Easter Sunday means a good brunch stop in Fresno is worth booking, and some places will be closed.

General Sherman Tree

30–45 minAbout 0.5 mi each wayPaved, some slope

By volume, the largest known living tree on Earth: about 275 feet tall and 36 feet across at the base. A paved trail leads down from the main lot, and the walk back up is the hard part. Late afternoon light is lovely, and the crowds thin out.

Check in for two nights

Wuksachi Lodge or Three Rivers

Wuksachi Lodge, inside the park and a few miles from Giant Forest, saves the most driving. Three Rivers, just outside the entrance, has more choice and lower prices but is about an hour each way.

Drive: Oakhurst to Giant Forest about 3 to 3.5 hrs, with the Generals Highway slow
Carry tire chains or check rental rules. Chain controls are common on the Generals Highway in March.

Day 6Mon, Mar 29

A full day in Giant Forest

Congress Trail

1.5–2 hrsAbout 3 mi loopMostly easy

Starting at the Sherman Tree, the Congress Trail winds through clusters of huge sequoias, including the President Tree and the McKinley Tree. Go early for quiet trails. Expect snow patches in March.

Giant Forest Museum and Big Trees Trail

1 hrAbout 1 mi, pavedEasy

The museum explains how sequoias live and depend on fire, and has good exhibits for kids. Across the road, the Big Trees Trail circles Round Meadow with sequoias at its edge. Ask the rangers here about Moro Rock and road conditions.

Moro Rock

45 min350+ steps, 0.5 mi round tripClosed if icy

A granite dome with a railed stairway to a big view of the Great Western Divide and the foothills. The steps are steep and exposed, so only go on clear, ice-free days. Younger kids need a hand.

Crescent Meadow and Tunnel Log

45 minShort walks

Along Crescent Meadow Road you can drive through Tunnel Log, a fallen sequoia with a car-sized opening. At the end, Crescent Meadow has a quiet loop. If the road is closed for snow, skip it or walk part of it.

Drive: short hops of 10 to 20 min around Giant Forest

Day 7Tue, Mar 30

Kings Canyon: Grant Grove, then Fresno

General Grant Grove

45–60 minAbout 0.3 mi loopPaved, easy

The General Grant Tree is the second-largest tree in the world by volume and the Nation's Christmas Tree. The loop passes the Fallen Monarch, a hollow log you can walk through, and the Gamlin Cabin.

North Grove Loop

1–1.5 hrsAbout 1.5 mi loopEasy to moderate

A quieter walk through big sequoias with fewer people. Expect snow patches and mud, so wear waterproof shoes.

Panoramic Point (if open)

~1 hr

The road is usually closed in winter and early spring. If it's open, a short walk leads to a wide view of the Sierra crest. Check at the Kings Canyon Visitor Center.

Down to Fresno

1.25–1.5 hrsStay near the airport

Highway 180 drops out of the sequoias into the Central Valley. Staying in Fresno tonight makes tomorrow's morning flights easy. Fill both cars with gas before you return them.

Drive: Giant Forest to Grant Grove about 1 hr · Grant Grove to Fresno 1.25 to 1.5 hrs
Cedar Grove and the canyon road below Grant Grove are normally closed until later in spring.

Late March reality check

Likely closed or limited

  • Glacier Point Road and Tioga Road (Yosemite)
  • Cedar Grove / Zumwalt Meadow (Kings Canyon)
  • Mariposa Grove shuttle (walk in from the lower lot)
  • Moro Rock stairs and Crescent Meadow Road if icy or snowy

Book or check early

  • Muir Woods parking or shuttle reservation for 9 people
  • Two one-way rental cars, SFO to Fresno
  • Lodging for Sat–Sun, Mar 27–28 (Easter weekend)
  • Park entry rules, road status and chain requirements the week before

Pack for

  • Layers, rain shells and warm hats for the groves
  • Waterproof shoes with grip
  • Snacks and walkie-talkies or a shared plan for two cars
  • Offline maps. Cell service is spotty

Why this works in spring

  • Waterfalls near peak flow
  • Fewer crowds than summer
  • Cool hiking weather in the groves
  • One-way route avoids a 7-hour backtrack
